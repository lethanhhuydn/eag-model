# Rubric for research-design competence in AI- and IoT-integrated STEM projects

Five components, four indicators each. Every indicator is scored on four levels:

| Level | Label | Generic descriptor |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Not yet evident | The element is missing, or what is written cannot guide an investigation. |
| 1 | Emerging | The element is present but incomplete, vague or only produced with substantial support. |
| 2 | Proficient | The element is complete, correct and produced independently; another student could act on it. |
| 3 | Advanced | As level 2, and the choice is justified against alternatives and its limitations are anticipated. |

Evidence comes from the design brief, the design log, the IoT configuration and data log, and the AI notebook or model card that students produce in each design cycle.

## 1. Problem framing

| Code | Indicator | What to look for |
|---|---|---|
| PF1 | Need or phenomenon | An authentic need or phenomenon is identified, with the people or systems affected. |
| PF2 | Investigable question or goal | A question that data can answer, or an engineering goal that a prototype can meet, is stated. |
| PF3 | Criteria and constraints | Measurable success criteria and constraints (cost, time, safety, ethics) are specified. |
| PF4 | Prior knowledge | The question is positioned against existing solutions or findings, and what is new is stated. |

## 2. System modelling

| Code | Indicator | What to look for |
|---|---|---|
| SM1 | Variables | Input, output and controlled variables (or factors and responses) are identified. |
| SM2 | Operational definitions | Each variable is defined as a measurable quantity with unit and range. |
| SM3 | Functional hypothesis | The expected relationship is expressed as a function, direction or quantitative prediction that data could contradict. |
| SM4 | System representation | A diagram shows the path from sensing to decision and where each variable is sensed and processed. |

## 3. Measurement design (IoT)

| Code | Indicator | What to look for |
|---|---|---|
| MD1 | Sensor choice | Sensors are selected whose range, resolution and response time match the operational definitions. |
| MD2 | Calibration and validity | Calibration against a reference is planned, with a check that the signal reflects the intended quantity. |
| MD3 | Sampling plan | Sampling rate, duration and placement capture the variation of interest; data loss is anticipated. |
| MD4 | Data pipeline and quality | Transmission, storage, time-stamping and quality checks (missing values, outliers, power, connectivity) are planned. |

## 4. Inference design (experimental logic and AI)

| Code | Indicator | What to look for |
|---|---|---|
| ID1 | Comparison and control | Conditions isolate the effect of interest (control of variables, baseline or benchmark). |
| ID2 | Replication and sample | Repeated trials or sufficient cases are planned and the amount of data is justified. |
| ID3 | Analysis or model plan | Analyses or a machine-learning model suited to the question are chosen before the data are seen; features, labels and metrics are specified. |
| ID4 | Validation and uncertainty | Training and test data are separated, or held-out trials are used; error, bias and uncertainty will be estimated and reported. |

## 5. Iterative refinement

| Code | Indicator | What to look for |
|---|---|---|
| IR1 | Prototype and test | A testable version is built early and tested against the criteria. |
| IR2 | Diagnosis | Test evidence is used to locate the cause of shortfalls (design, measurement or model). |
| IR3 | Trade-offs and decisions | Alternatives are weighed against criteria and constraints, and the chosen revision is justified. |
| IR4 | Documentation and responsibility | A dated design log would let others repeat the work; safety, privacy and the ethical use of data and AI are addressed. |

## Holistic design review

At the end of each design cycle two raters independently give one overall score from 0 to 100 for the quality of the research design as a whole, using the bands below. Raters do not add up the analytic indicators.

| Band | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 0 to 20 | No workable design. |
| 21 to 40 | Fragments of a design; an investigation could not be carried out from it. |
| 41 to 60 | A workable design with clear weaknesses that threaten the conclusions. |
| 61 to 80 | A sound design that would yield interpretable evidence. |
| 81 to 100 | A design ready for an external science and engineering fair. |

## Alignment

| Component | Practices in the Framework for K-12 Science Education | Regeneron ISEF judging criteria |
|---|---|---|
| Problem framing | Asking questions and defining problems | Research question or research problem |
| System modelling | Developing and using models; using mathematics and computational thinking | Design and methodology |
| Measurement design | Planning and carrying out investigations | Design and methodology; execution |
| Inference design | Planning investigations; analysing and interpreting data | Design and methodology; execution |
| Iterative refinement | Constructing explanations and designing solutions | Execution; creativity |
